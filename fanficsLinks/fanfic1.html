Amaria

Amaria wasn’t just a gamer girl; she was the gamer girl. Her unmatched skills in Rainbow Six Siege earned her a reputation in the online world, and her sharp wit in the chat always kept her teammates entertained. But beneath the persona of Amaria was Eggwosh, a guy who enjoyed slipping into a different identity to avoid the judgment and pressures of the world. When Gio joined her squad a few months ago, their dynamic was instant magic. He admired her skill, her humor, and the mysterious charm she exuded—even if they had never spoken beyond game chat.

Gio found himself logging on more and more, not just to win matches but to hear Amaria's voice in his headset, playful and focused. He didn’t know much about her, but he knew enough to feel something stirring in him—a crush, maybe even something stronger. One late night during a grueling match, while they were pinned in a virtual firefight, Gio blurted out, “Hey, Amaria, can I... I mean, do you think we could talk sometime? Like... actually talk?” His words stumbled, much like his in-game movements when nervous. Amaria hesitated, her usual confidence replaced by awkward silence. “I don’t know, Gio. I’m... really shy,” she finally replied. “It’s not easy for me, you know?”

Gio didn’t push. But over the next few weeks, his gentle persistence—kind words and friendly banter—chipped away at her reluctance. Eggwosh, behind the screen, wrestled with nerves. Could he really keep up this façade forever? It wasn’t about deceit—it had started as fun, but Gio’s warmth had made Eggwosh feel guilty. One evening, after a long match, Eggwosh made a decision. “Hey, Gio... I think I’m ready to call you. Just don’t laugh if my voice is weird, okay?”

The phone rang, and Gio’s heart raced as he picked up. “Hello?” came a voice—soft, low, and unmistakably... masculine. There was a beat of silence on Gio’s end. “Wait... Amaria?” he asked cautiously. Eggwosh, feeling a rush of panic and relief all at once, laughed nervously. “Yeah... well, kind of. My real name’s Eggwosh. I’ve been playing as Amaria, but... yeah, surprise, I’m a dude.” Gio didn’t respond right away, and Eggwosh braced himself for anger, or maybe even disgust. Instead, Gio chuckled. “Well, that explains why you never used a mic. Honestly... I’m just glad I can finally put a voice to the teammate who’s been carrying me all this time.”

From that moment on, their friendship deepened. They played together more than ever, sharing stories about their lives outside the game. Eggwosh found himself opening up to Gio in a way he hadn’t with anyone else, and Gio—though he tried to suppress it—still felt a flicker of those early romantic feelings. Sometimes, when Eggwosh cracked a joke or pulled off an impossible headshot, Gio caught himself smiling a little too much. But instead of dwelling on what could’ve been, he embraced the bond they had now: a friendship built on trust, laughter, and endless late-night gaming sessions.

And though neither of them said it aloud, in their own way, they had both found something special—proof that connection didn’t need labels to mean everything
