Las Vegas


The lights of Las Vegas shimmered like restless stars against the desert sky, but Eggwosh’s hands trembled for a reason beyond the neon glow. They clutched the handle of their modest suitcase as though it were a lifeline. A few steps away, Mori stood beneath the flickering sign of the "Golden Mirage Casino," waiting with a smirk that hinted at mischief. Eggwosh had never been a fan of big cities—or gambling, for that matter—but when Mori called, the world always seemed smaller, friendlier, and brimming with the promise of adventure. “Ready to win big?” Mori teased, slinging an arm around Eggwosh’s shoulder. The warmth of the gesture sent an unexpected flutter through Eggwosh’s chest. They laughed nervously, waving it off. “I’m just here to keep you out of trouble,” Eggwosh quipped, though they knew Mori would likely drag them headlong into it. As they walked toward the casino doors, a bright fruit-themed display of slot machines made Eggwosh freeze. Among the cheerful spinning cherries and oranges sat the unmistakable visage of a kiwi. They shuddered, taking an instinctive step back. Mori noticed. “Hey, you okay?” Their voice softened, their hand brushing Eggwosh’s. It was a small gesture, but enough to draw them back. “I hate kiwis,” Eggwosh admitted with a sheepish laugh. Mori tilted their head, concerned but amused. “The fruit?” Eggwosh nodded, their voice dropping. “They’re fuzzy, green, and… evil. Don’t ask.” Mori grinned. “Noted. No kiwis. Now, come on—roulette waits for no one!”

later, they found themselves richer by $200 and poorer by three cocktails each. The night felt alive, as though it were just for them, and as they stumbled onto the quiet Vegas strip, Mori turned to Eggwosh, their voice unusually serious. “You know, I’m glad we did this. There’s something about tonight… It feels different.” Eggwosh blushed, unsure if it was the lingering buzz of the drinks or Mori’s tone that made their heart skip. Before they could respond, Mori grinned and stepped closer. “Let me write you a poem.”


Beneath the lights, the city glows,
But what’s bright pales to what I know.
The warmth of you, the laugh you share,
Like a gambler’s chance, beyond compare.

Though fears of kiwis you may keep,
And risk the night, awake, not asleep,
I’ll wager my heart, in a city of play,
That you’re the treasure I’d bet on any day.

Eggwosh blinked, trying to hide their smile behind their hand. “You’re such a sap.” But their voice betrayed them, thick with fondness. Mori chuckled, taking their hand again. “Let’s go watch the sunrise,” they said. And for once, Eggwosh didn’t feel the fear of big cities or kiwis or the risk of taking a gamble on something bigger. After all, with Mori, every adventure felt like the safest bet.
