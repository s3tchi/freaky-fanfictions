Subway day><

mr perez stepped off the bus, gripping his phone with trembling hands as he texted, “Where even IS this subway, Yesseniugh?” The tall buildings of Yesseniugh’s hometown loomed above him, unfamiliar and intimidating. mr perez, new to the city and hopeless with directions, felt lost in the chaos of honking cars and bustling pedestrians. Yesseniugh, his clumsy but ever-demanding best of best fwren, responded with her usual unhelpful energy: “It’s literally five blocks from my place! Use your eyes, hampster !!” mr perez sighed. “hampster ” was her nickname for him, one that annoyed him slightly but also made him smile. After wandering aimlessly and asking a hot dog vendor for directions (who promptly ignored him), mr perez finally stumbled upon Yesseniugh at a corner near her apartment. “You’re late!” she said, waving her arms dramatically and almost knocking over a streetlamp. “I’m starving! Let’s go get this sandwich before I faint!” Despite her impatience, she offered him a warm smile that melted his irritation. Together, they made their way to the Subway, Yesseniugh talking non-stop about her latest escapade: tripping over her own shoelaces and accidentally knocking over a display of energy drinks at the grocery store. mr perez chuckled, feeling more at ease with her familiar chaos.

Inside the Subway, the two stood at the counter, debating their order. “We’re getting the 12-inch rotisserie chicken bacon avocado with creamy sriracha,” Yesseniugh declared with authority. mr perez nodded in agreement until they reached the part about vegetables. “Load it up with all the veggies!” he said eagerly. But Yesseniugh gasped in horror. “ALL the veggies?! Are you insane? No, just lettuce and spinach. That’s all you need!” mr perez crossed his arms. “That’s barely any vegetables. You’re ruining the sandwich!” Yesseniugh huffed and stomped her foot, accidentally stepping on mr perez’s. “I don’t care! I’m not eating tomatoes or cucumbers. They’re gross!” The sandwich artist behind the counter raised an eyebrow, waiting patiently as they bickered.

Eventually, they reached a compromise: one half loaded with vegetables for mr perez and the other with just lettuce and spinach for Yesseniugh. They grabbed their sandwich and headed to a nearby pharmacy building with a rooftop view. As the sun began to set, they climbed up a fire escape to the roof and sat together, legs dangling over the edge. The parking lot below glittered with fading sunlight, and the warm colors of dusk painted the sky. They passed the sandwich back and forth, laughing about their earlier fight and swapping bites of their respective halves.

Yesseniugh leaned back, smiling as she watched the sun dip below the horizon. “You know,” she said softly, “this was actually kind of nice. Even though you’re the most annoying person I know, hampster .” mr perez rolled his eyes but smiled. “Thanks, Yes. You’re not so bad yourself, even if you do have questionable vegetable opinions.” They laughed, their voices carrying into the evening air, the warmth of their friendship making the simple meal on a pharmacy rooftop feel like the greatest adventure.
